National Geographic Kids

A clearer, more playful site for young explorers

A redesign concept for National Geographic Kids that simplifies navigation, cuts visual clutter and makes learning feel like play for children aged 6–12, while keeping parents' trust.

Problem

What the existing experience revealed

Cluttered homepage

Competing visuals, banners and tiles overwhelm young users and prevent focused exploration.

No age or interest filtering

Children 6–12 have different needs, but there is no way to tailor content by age or topic.

Inconsistent layout

Irregular type, tile sizes and button styles create friction and reduce trust.

Poor mobile experience

Small touch targets and broken layouts on small screens shut out mobile‑first learners.

Research

Key insights

What I did
  • Heuristic evaluation of the current site from UX and UI perspectives
  • Analyzed 6 competitors: Raz‑Kids, Epic, PBS Kids, Discovery Kids, BBC Earth Kids, NWF Kids
  • Mapped navigation, visual hierarchy and accessibility gaps
  • Prioritized features with the MoSCoW method
What I found
  • Visual clutter reduces time on page and increases drop‑off
  • Kids need 5 clear top‑level categories: Animals, Games, Videos, Explore, Quiz
  • Epic and Raz‑Kids show that simpler UI increases engagement
  • Mobile responsiveness lagged behind every competitor
  • Gamification and progress indicators drive return visits

Principles

Principles behind the solution

Design for the child first

Simple language, large tap targets and bright but organized visuals that match how children explore.

Reduce friction at every step

Clear navigation, no intrusive pop‑ups and fast access to content without sign‑up barriers.

Make learning feel like play

Quizzes, games and videos get equal visual weight so exploring feels fun, not like homework.

Solution

Design solution

Simplified navigation + homepage
Feature 01

Simplified navigation + homepage

Top navigation restructured into 5 categories. The homepage gets a focused hero, a category picker and featured animals, guiding kids straight to what they love.

Pick Your Adventure
Feature 02

Pick Your Adventure

An age‑aware entry point where kids choose Wild Animals, Fun Games, Cool Videos or Science Fun, which reduces overwhelm and surfaces relevant content quickly.

Play & Learn hub
Feature 03

Play & Learn hub

A quiz and game section with Animal Quiz, Geography, Ocean Life and Insects, using clear calls to action and consistent cards so kids can jump in and come back.

Impact

Impact and results

Clear navigation

Animals, Games, Videos, Explore and Quiz replace an overwhelming homepage with a 5‑choice menu.

Less visual clutter

A focused hero, category cards and featured animals remove competing noise.

Mobile‑first layout

Touch‑friendly cards, larger tap targets and a responsive layout for any device.

User flow

From landing to learning

A 6‑step path from first visit to active learning. The content discovery moment is the key interaction.

  1. Home
  2. Pick Your Adventure
  3. Browse category
  4. View content
  5. Quiz / game
  6. Explore more

Reflection

Conclusion

Redesigning National Geographic Kids showed me that clarity and delight have to coexist in children's UX. The central challenge was cutting visual noise while keeping the brand's adventurous spirit, and every decision had to serve both the child's curiosity and the parent's trust.

Next, I would run usability tests with children aged 6–12 and measure session length, quiz completion and navigation drop‑off before and after the redesign.
