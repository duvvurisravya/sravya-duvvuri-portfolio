Branding & Visual Identity Design · MA final project

5City Farmers Nursery

Rooted in nature. Growing together.

City Farmers Nursery is a locally rooted garden center in San Diego that blends plant retail, education and hands‑on experiences. I designed a unified brand identity and a four‑page website so the experience feels as welcoming online as it does in person.

Desktop prototype ↗Mobile prototype ↗
ProjectSDSU Art 541 · Project 2
InstructorProfessor James Bowman
ScopeBrand identity · Brand standards · Website
LocationSan Diego, CA
Problem

A rich space, an inconsistent brand

The physical space offers a rich, community‑driven environment, but the brand doesn't carry that experience across its visual identity and digital touchpoints.

Inconsistent visual identity

The brand lacks consistency across its visual identity, so the nursery looks different from one touchpoint to the next.

Disconnected digital touchpoints

The in‑person experience isn't communicated online, which leaves a gap between the space and how people find it.

Website out of step with the store

Long‑time customers noticed the website doesn't reflect what's actually in store.

Events are hard to find

Families couldn't find clear information about workshops and events.

Brand strategy

What the brand stands for

Mission · “Grow Together”

A shared space where people connect with plants, learn through experience and build a sense of community.

Positioning

An accessible, community‑first destination that combines gardening, education and family‑friendly experiences, a more engaging and locally connected alternative to traditional nurseries and large retail chains.

Purpose

To inspire greener lives and stronger communities through plants, knowledge and meaningful connections.

Brand attributes

Organic

Natural, textured, non‑corporate feel

Welcoming

Inclusive for all experience levels

Educational

Learning‑driven interactions

Local

Strong community roots

Playful

Family‑friendly and exploratory

Sustainable

Environmentally conscious practices

Personas

Who the brand serves

Three customers, from a first‑time plant owner to a long‑time regular.

1 SofiaUrban plant beginner25 · Apartment living · North Park / Hillcrest

A recent SDSU graduate working in tech. She follows plant content online but lacks confidence in plant care, and prefers approachable, affordable options over high‑end boutique shops.

Goals
  • Add greenery to her apartment
  • Start with low‑maintenance plants
Needs
  • Simple, clear guidance from staff
  • Easy‑to‑follow plant care information
Frustrations
  • Difficulty keeping plants alive
  • Boutique plant shops feel expensive and unapproachable
“I want plants in my space, but I need help knowing how to take care of them.”
2 MarcusFamily gardener42 · Homeowner · La Mesa / El Cajon

A middle school teacher and father of two who gardens at home and values experiences that let his children learn about nature hands‑on.

Goals
  • Teach his children about gardening and sustainability
  • Grow vegetables and herbs at home
Needs
  • A family‑friendly environment and activities
  • Access to workshops and educational resources
Frustrations
  • Few family‑oriented garden destinations
  • No clear information about events and workshops
“I want a place where my kids can learn and enjoy being outdoors.”
3 LindaDevoted hobbyist gardener58 · Homeowner · College Area / Kensington

A retired landscape designer and long‑time customer who visits often and values the nursery's plant variety, expertise and connection to the local community.

Goals
  • Find unique, high‑quality plants
  • Keep developing her gardening knowledge
Needs
  • Access to knowledgeable staff
  • A wide selection of specialized plants
Frustrations
  • The website doesn't reflect in‑store inventory
  • Worry that future changes may reduce authenticity
“This place has always been special. I value its authenticity and expertise.”

Brand standards

Keeping the mark consistent

Minimum sizes

Stacked logo 1.5 in (38 mm / 60 px). Horizontal logo 2.0 in (51 mm / 80 px). Icon only 0.75 in (19 mm / 40 px).

Clear space

Keep 0.5 in of clear space around the logo so it stays balanced and easy to recognise.

Incorrect uses

Don't stretch, rotate, add effects, change colours, remove elements or swap the typeface, and avoid busy backgrounds.
